# Tether

A native iPhone and iPad zodiac observatory using the clearly synthetic **Alex and Sam** sample profiles. SwiftUI presents iOS 27 Liquid Glass controls, Metal draws the orbital and time projections, Core Haptics supplies tactile feedback, and WidgetKit delivers daily personal reflections. The architecture follows the existing **Orbitals** app; there is no web view or JavaScript runtime.

## Run

Open `Tether.xcodeproj` in **Xcode 27**, select the **Tether** scheme and an iOS 27 simulator, then Run. The minimum target is iOS/iPadOS 27.0. The generated project is included; use `xcodegen generate` after changing the source inventory.

The application identifier is `com.utkarsh.tether`, with widget extension `com.utkarsh.tether.widgets`. The app and widget share the `group.com.utkarsh.tether` App Group. Development team settings follow Orbitals. A physical-device install needs a provisioning profile that includes this new App Group; this repository does not constitute an App Store or TestFlight release.

## Four spaces

- **Reading:** choose Alex or Sam and read an original interpretation for the selected instant. Every selected transit exposes the bodies, aspect, measured separation, orb, and applying/separating state.
- **Explore:** 2D ecliptic, 3D orbits, 4D worldlines, 5D time cylinder, time torus, and orbital tracks. Drag to rotate, pinch to zoom, double-tap to reset. The calendar sheet supports a precise UT date, one-day steps, birth-date jumps, Now, and day/month/year/200-year scrubber scales. Playback updates the same clock used by readings and calendars.
- **Us:** a shared reflection, two persistent birth anchors, natal placements, and birth-time uncertainty. The aquamarine/rose tether is an artistic metaphor for connection.
- **Cycles:** an Earth-view Moon phase and synchronized solar/lunar dial, tithi, nakshatra, Lahiri rashis, next lunar/solar event jumps, and an independently traversable traditional Hindu timescale from yugas to a Kalpa.

Touch and hold the Home Screen, choose **Edit → Add Widget**, and search for **Tether**. Small and medium widgets can each be configured for either person. A daily reading is evaluated at **local noon** on that date; tapping the widget opens that person and exact instant. WidgetKit controls when scheduled entries appear. The app supplies the current entry plus seven upcoming local-calendar days, respecting daylight-saving calendar boundaries.

## What is calculated—and what is interpreted

The app bundles the same verified **NASA/JPL Horizons 1900–2100** samples as Orbital Atlas. Cubic Hermite interpolation reconstructs position and velocity; the Moon has its own Earth-centered, six-hour cache. The app refuses to extrapolate outside this interval. “Every alignment ever” is not a scientifically supportable exact claim: all ephemerides have finite coverage and uncertainty.

Visual orbits are heliocentric. Readings use **geocentric geometric mean ecliptic longitudes of date**: Sun = −Earth, planets = planet − Earth, and Moon already Earth-centered. The precession transform is adapted from IAU SOFA, whose license is bundled. Tropical and mean Lahiri sidereal references are available. Nutation, aberration, and light-time corrections are omitted, so lunar/solar event buttons show geometric estimates rather than exact apparent almanac times.

The bundled publication profiles are synthetic: Alex uses 1 January 2000 at 12:00 UTC; Sam uses 1 July 2001 at 12:00 in a fixed UTC−05:00 zone. Their sample cities and times do not describe real people. Settings can record another local time; houses and ascendants remain omitted. The app displays Moon/nakshatra possibilities across the full selected date.

Readings are generated by an **original deterministic interpretation model**, not downloaded from Co-Star. The model compares transiting and natal positions using conjunction/opposition (6° orb), square/trine (5°), and sextile (3°). It ranks matches by normalized angular closeness, selects five, and composes original reflection text with an explicit fallback. These orbs and meanings are editorial conventions, not probabilities. Astrology is not scientifically established as a predictor of personality or events. No horoscope corpus, cloud model, account, subscription, or API key is needed.

The 4D/5D labels identify time projections and exploratory lenses, not additional demonstrated physical dimensions or physical time travel. A torus reference cage is closed; real orbit samples retain their distinct endpoints. Near the supported date boundaries, the symmetric time window shrinks to available coverage. Planetary paths and node distances are artistically rescaled for legibility. The Moon’s visual separation is enlarged. Birth nodes remain visible before and after their birth dates as symbolic reference directions.

Traditional Hindu chronology uses the adopted PAC Kali epoch, **18 February 3102 BCE at 00:00 IST in the proleptic Julian calendar**, and a 365.25-day display year. Yugas, Mahayugas, Manvantaras, sandhyas, and Kalpas have their stated traditional lengths. This separate clock never requests unsupported planetary positions. Instantaneous tithi and nakshatra are not a complete location-dependent festival calendar.

## Privacy and efficiency

Everything runs offline after installation. Birth times and zodiac preference are stored locally in the app/widget App Group. There is no analytics or external horoscope service. Only the active native canvas draws; paused scenes redraw on state changes, Metal uses three bounded reusable buffers, and backgrounding stops playback without time catch-up. Binary ephemeris tables are memory-mapped. Haptics are causal and rate-limited; physical feel must be checked on a real iPhone.
